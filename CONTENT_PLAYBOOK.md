### The patterns that repeat (and that you'll copy)
1. **Hook = first 1–2 lines, under ~12 words, with a number, a contradiction, or a confession.** "3 of my 8 AI agents don't use AI." "A driver replied 👍. My agent refused to guess."
2. **One idea per post.** Story → lesson → specifics → question (Lara's SLAY: Story, Lesson, Actionable, You).
3. **Short lines, white space, arrow or numbered lists.** It reads in about 30–45 seconds, which feeds dwell time.
4. **Real numbers only.** Every top post above has a specific number in the first 3 lines.
5. **Mix about 80% "here's how/what I built" with about 20% personal story.**
6. **End with a specific, easy question** ("Builders: how do you handle ambiguous replies?"), not "Thoughts?" and not "Comment X".
7. **Consistency over virality.** Lara's 129-impression week happened to her too.

### What does NOT transfer to a 71-follower account (don't copy these yet)
- "Repost ♻️ if…" asks, newsletter funnels, "the best 30 comments get a coffee chat", product launches: these need an audience first.
- Long 22-item listicles of tool news: these need authority first. Your edge is **your own builds**, which they can't copy.
- Hype openers ("OMG", "🚨", "This is insane"). Your old "OMG… $2,000+ funnel" post got 82 impressions.

## 5. Your voice rules (builder, not marketer)

- **Write like an engineer's build log, not an ad.** "I built X. Here's the decision that mattered." Never "Stop losing money!"
- First person, plain words, short sentences. At most one emoji per post, and only if it's part of the story (the 👍).
- Always say **what it can't do** or **what broke**. That's your signature and the thing that builds trust.
- Say "built", not "deployed for clients". Say "synthetic data" when it is. Say "open code" when it is.
- **0–3 hashtags**, at the end. Links **only in the first comment**.
- 900–1,400 characters for text posts. Carousels: 6–8 slides with a short caption.
- Don't edit a post in the first hour.

### Amplification: your biggest lever (proven by the L&T Finance post)
- **Tag only people who were genuinely part of the story:** judges, organisers, teammates, mentors, clubs, a tool's maker when you used it seriously. 2–5 tags at most, placed naturally in the text (a "thank you" line). Never tag strangers for reach; LinkedIn treats tag-spam like engagement bait, and people untag or mute you.
- **Give them a reason to repost:** the post should make *them* look good (their event, their mentorship, their club's member winning).
- **Send a short DM after posting:** "Thank you again for {X}. I wrote about it here, in case it's useful for the {club/event} page." Don't ask "please repost"; make it easy and let them choose.
- **NITK is a distribution channel:** clubs (IET NITK, Incub8 NITK, your hackathon/AI clubs), batchmates and seniors. Share wins in the relevant group chats with the link.
- **Never tag** the MedLog interviewee or his employer (anonymity rule).

## 7. Cadence: how to "blast more" without killing your own reach

You want volume. The data says **2 posts on the same day each lose about 40% reach**. So the volume goes here:
